What we are looking for

A sleep spindle is a burst of 11–16 Hz waves lasting about half a second to two seconds, in light (N2) sleep. Below is a real 40-second stretch of one night. Under it, each row is one detector's answer, and the grey bands show where at least two of the four agree.

02

Experts marked very little of it

Drawing spindles by hand is slow, so only two small datasets have expert marks. Everything else has sleep stages but no spindles. That imbalance is the reason for Part B.

code: spindlebench download · spindlebench cache → cache/manifest.csv
03

One referee for every detector

Every detector is wrapped so it reads the same files and writes the same table of events. One scoring code then compares each with the experts, using the same rules for all. Changing a rule changes the ranking, which is what the benchmark studies.

code: detectors/common.py · experiments/rq1_benchmark/evaluate.py · src/spindlebench/protocols/benchmark.py
Part B · can a detector learn without any expert?
04

Four teachers with different habits

Four rule-based detectors act as teachers. None of them ever saw an expert label. They agree on only 34%–57% of each other's events, and each has a measurable habit.

numbers: results/student/teachers/density.csv · injection/teacher_recall.csv · work/targets/label_models.json
05

First, clean the nights

Movement and loose electrodes create huge bursts. Detectors that compare each moment with the whole night's level then set their bar too high and go almost silent. About 1% of each night is flagged and every teacher works on the rest.

code: spindlebench artefacts (artefacts.v1) · detectors/common.py --clean
06

Votes become lessons

At every 10 ms of the 1,200 hours of unlabelled N2 sleep, the four teachers either fire or not: 16 possible patterns. Each pattern becomes a target for the student, here the simple vote and the Dawid–Skene estimate, which weighs teachers by how reliable they look.

code: experiments/student/rasterise.py · label_models.py fit
07

The student

The part that stays is a small U-Net, exactly the network of SUMOv2 (a published, expert-trained detector), but trained from scratch on teacher votes. During training it gets two helpers: glasses that see what the teachers measure, and a profiler that learns where each teacher goes wrong.

code: experiments/student/model.py · details: docs/student_architecture.md
08

How it learns

Training repeats a short loop thousands of times. The key step is 3: the student is not blamed for disagreeing with a teacher who is known to miss things.

code: experiments/student/train.py · losses.py · validate.py · pilot now: pass 8, agreement with the vote 0.64 (best so far 0.70)
09

Testing without answers

To check the teachers without experts, we plant artificial spindles of known strength into real nights and count how many each teacher finds. The method needs the average to pass 50%. It does from 9 dB (52%); weaker spindles are invisible to all four, so the student cannot learn them.

code: experiments/student/build_val_injection.py · score_val_injection.py
10

The final exam

The expert-labelled sets stay locked until the student is chosen using unlabelled nights alone. Then it runs once and is scored by the same referee as every detector in Part A.

code: data.guard() refuses DREAMS and NAP · select.py → frozen.json · infer.py
